## Help someone understand your work before they click away

A designer's link-in-bio page can be a compact introduction to a larger portfolio. Show selected work, explain what you do, and give visitors a clear next step. They should not have to open five different profiles to understand your focus.

Grabbin's visual blocks let you put project images beside links and short notes. You can arrange desktop and mobile layouts separately, so the same work has a useful reading order on both.

The structure below is an illustrative page plan, not a customer case study or a claim about hiring results. Explore the [Grabbin demo](/demo) to see a sample page in use.

## A practical first-page structure

Imagine a product designer who wants visitors to explore two projects and then get in touch. Their page could contain these six pieces:

1. **Introduction:** a name, profile image, and one sentence about the kind of design work they do.
2. **Featured project image:** a clear view of the work they most want to discuss.
3. **Project link:** a destination with the full case study, including the process and their role.
4. **Second project:** a different example that adds something the first does not show.
5. **Availability note:** an accurate description of the work they are currently open to.
6. **Contact destination:** a link to the page or profile where they accept inquiries.

Treat this as a starting point. An illustrator may lead with images from a series, while a motion designer may choose a short video. Select the work around the type of conversation you want to have.

## Give each project enough context

A polished image shows craft, but it may not explain your contribution. Place a short text block near the project or make the linked case study easy to identify.

For a fictional checkout project, a useful note could be: “Mobile checkout concept. I worked on the interaction flow and interface design.” That tells the reader what they are looking at without inventing business results.

If you have measured outcomes and permission to share them, include the source and context in the full case study. Otherwise, describe the work and your role plainly. Use only client materials you are allowed to publish.

## Arrange the desktop layout

Start with your introduction, then give the featured project a larger block. Keep its explanation and case-study link nearby so the relationship is clear. Place secondary work after it, followed by your availability and contact destination.

Avoid making every block equally prominent. A social profile does not always need as much room as the project that explains your skills. Section headings such as “Selected work” and “Find me elsewhere” can separate these groups.

## Give the mobile layout its own reading order

Switch to the mobile layout in the editor and check the sequence from top to bottom:

1. Who you are and what you do.
2. Your featured project and its context.
3. A second piece of work.
4. How to contact you.
5. Additional profiles or personal interests.

Adjust image crops so the important detail survives the smaller frame. Do not let a long group of social links push all your work out of the first part of the page. Open the public page on your phone before sharing it.

## Keep your full case studies where they work best

You do not have to move an established portfolio into your bio page. Link to existing case studies on your own website or another platform, and use Grabbin to give people a clear starting point.

If a project needs a long explanation, detailed process images, or an extended walkthrough, keep that detail in the linked case study. Your bio page can explain why it is worth opening.

## Choose a personal address when you need one

Your Grabbin handle is enough to share the page. Pro also lets you connect a subdomain you own, such as `portfolio.example.com`. Root domains are not supported.

Read [how to connect a custom domain](/update/connect-custom-domain) for the requirements and setup steps. The custom address displays your public page; editing stays at your original Grabbin address.

## Review before you share

Ask someone unfamiliar with your work to open the page. Can they tell what you do, find a project, and locate your contact destination without explanation? Their questions can reveal missing context that is hard to notice in your own layout.

Recheck the page when your availability changes or a project link moves. A small selection of current work is easier to maintain than a page containing every project you have ever made.
