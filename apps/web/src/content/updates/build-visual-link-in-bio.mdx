## Start with what you want people to find

A visual link-in-bio page brings your links and work together at one address. With Grabbin, you can add links, photos, videos, text, and places, then arrange them as blocks instead of presenting everything as a list of buttons.

For a first page, gather a profile image, a short introduction, two or three useful links, and a few images or videos you have permission to share. You do not need to fill every part of the page.

Open the [interactive demo](/demo) if you want to explore a sample before you start. It is an example page, not a customer testimonial.

## 1. Create your page and introduce yourself

[Sign in](/sign-in) and choose a handle when creating your first page. Your handle gives you an address to share. If you already have a page, open it while signed in to continue editing.

Add your name, a profile image, and a short introduction. Explain what visitors can find here. For example, a photographer might write: “Portraits, recent shoots, and places that inspire my work.” Use your own description rather than leaving sample text in place.

## 2. Add the links people need

Use the editor's link field to add a destination, such as your portfolio, a social profile, or a project. Check the resulting preview: its title and image should help someone recognize what they will open.

Start with the links that support the page's purpose. A page for work inquiries may need a portfolio and a contact destination; a personal page may lead with photos and a favorite project.

## 3. Show your work with photos and videos

Add media so visitors can see what you make before following a link. Pick an image that remains understandable at a small size. For a project screenshot, avoid a crop that cuts off the part you want people to notice.

To adjust an image on desktop, hover over the media block, choose **Crop**, and drag the image to change its frame.

<UpdateBlockPreview type="media" />

Give a piece of work some context with a nearby text block: what it is, what you contributed, and where someone can learn more. Do not rely on an image alone to explain the project.

## 4. Arrange the page around one clear starting point

Drag blocks into place and choose sizes that suit their content. Give your main project more room, then place supporting links nearby. Use section headings when the page needs distinct groups, such as “Selected work” and “Elsewhere.”

Text blocks work well for short introductions or notes. A map can add context for a public studio or a place you want to recommend; you do not need to add a location just to complete the layout.

## 5. Check the mobile layout separately

Switch between desktop and mobile layouts while editing on your computer. Grabbin lets you edit the mobile layout there too.

Check the reading order again on the narrower layout. Keep your introduction and most useful work easy to find, and make sure images still have useful crops. A desktop arrangement is not a substitute for checking the phone version.

## 6. Wait for saving, then check the public page

Grabbin saves your edits automatically. Let saving finish before closing the page. If an error appears, resolve it before assuming the latest change is available to visitors.

Open the shared address in a private window and check:

- Your name and introduction are correct.
- Each link opens the intended destination.
- Photos and videos load.
- The layout makes sense on a phone as well as a larger screen.
- Sample copy and content you do not intend to share are gone.

## 7. Share one address

Use **Share Link** to copy your page link or share a QR code. Add the address to your social profile, email signature, or wherever people ask to see your work.

You can start with a free page. If you want to use a subdomain you own, follow the [custom domain guide](/update/connect-custom-domain); that feature requires Pro.

For a broader look at the available blocks, see [What Grabbin Gives You](/update/what-grabbin-gives-you). When you are ready, [create your page](/sign-in).
