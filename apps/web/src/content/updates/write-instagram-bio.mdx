## Write for someone who has not met you

The strongest Instagram bio tells a new visitor three things quickly: who the account belongs to, what the person makes or does, and what to open next. You do not need to explain your entire career in a few lines.

Draft one sentence using this pattern: “I’m a [role] making [type of work] for [audience or purpose].” If the audience is unclear, use a subject or medium instead: “Illustrator working in editorial and picture books.” Only use an industry label that describes your actual work.

## Add a useful next step

Choose the action that matches your current goal. A portfolio link can lead to selected projects. A newsletter link can lead to a signup page. A contact link can lead to the inbox or form you already use. Avoid “DM for details” if you do not want to handle messages that way.

If you have several destinations, use clear labels on your page: “Selected projects,” “Latest release,” or “Contact.” A visual page can add images and a short description beside those links; a simple direct profile link can be enough when there is only one destination.

## Edit with a small checklist

Before saving, ask:

1. Can a first-time visitor tell what I do?
2. Does the first link match the bio's promise?
3. Is every claim current and accurate?
4. Can a visitor reach my work or contact route in one or two taps?

Remove vague phrases that could describe any account. Emojis can help divide information, but they should not make the meaning harder to scan. Use the [Instagram bio idea prompts](/update/instagram-bio-ideas) if the first line is still difficult to write.

## Match the profile with the destination

If your bio introduces a photographer, show photography first. If you are a writer, lead with a current piece or a useful archive. Grabbin can organize a profile page from text, images, videos, places, and links. Your full portfolio, email form, or booking service can remain where it already works; the page can point people there.

Review your profile on a phone after editing, because spacing and line breaks may display differently from the editor. For a walkthrough of adding the destination, see [a link in bio for Instagram](/update/link-in-bio-instagram).
