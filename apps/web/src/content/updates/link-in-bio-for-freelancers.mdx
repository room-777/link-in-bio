## Make it easy for a potential client to decide what to do next

A freelancer link-in-bio page should answer three practical questions: what do you do, is your work a fit, and how can someone contact you? A list of every profile you use may contain the answers, but a visitor should not have to piece them together.

Grabbin lets you arrange links, photos, videos, and text as visual blocks on one page. Use it as a starting point that sends people to your full portfolio, project details, and the contact method you already use. It does not replace an inquiry form, booking tool, or contract process.

## Start with a short description of your work

Write one or two sentences that say what you do and who you do it for. A web developer might say, “I build small business websites and help teams improve the pages they already have.” A photographer might name a specialty and service area.

Use wording that describes the work you actually take on. Avoid broad labels such as “creative solutions” when a plain description will help a client decide whether to keep reading.

## Show a small selection of relevant projects

Choose two or three projects that match the kind of work you want more of. For each one, include an image or video when it helps people understand the result, then add a short note with:

- What the project was.
- What you did.
- Who the work was for, if you have permission to name them.
- A link to the full project or case study.

Do not add client names, results, or testimonials unless you can support and share them. If a project is confidential, use a different example or describe the type of work without identifying the client.

For a visual portfolio layout focused on design work, see the [designer page guide](/update/link-in-bio-for-designers). The same idea applies across freelance work: give each example enough context to explain why it belongs on the page.

## Explain the service before listing every tool you use

Potential clients usually need to understand the service before they need your software list. If your services have clear boundaries, name them in a short text block. For example, a copywriter might list website copy, editing, and messaging reviews. A video editor might link to a reel and note the formats they work with.

Mention useful limits or preferences, such as the kind of project you accept or the region where you work. Keep details current. If you do not publish rates, you can leave them out and describe how a visitor should ask about a project instead.

## Give visitors one clear contact path

Add the destination where you want new inquiries to go. That could be an email address, an existing contact form, or a professional profile with messaging enabled. Grabbin links to the destination; it does not collect inquiry details itself.

If you use an external calendar or booking page, make that clear in the link label. Do not make a visitor guess whether a button starts a conversation, opens a calendar, or leads to a general profile.

## Put the information in a useful order

Arrange the page around the client's decision:

1. A brief introduction and service description.
2. One strong, relevant project with context.
3. Supporting examples and links to full case studies.
4. Your availability or a note about the work you accept.
5. The contact destination.

Use section headings if they make groups easier to scan, such as “Selected work” or “Services.” Give the most relevant project more visual space; not every social profile needs equal emphasis.

Check the mobile layout separately. Read from top to bottom and make sure the introduction, work, and contact path appear in an order that still makes sense on a narrow screen. The [visual page building guide](/update/build-visual-link-in-bio) walks through adding blocks, checking layouts, and sharing your page.

## Keep the page current

Replace outdated project links, remove work you no longer have permission to show, and update your availability when it changes. Open the public page after editing and test each destination. If you share the page across profiles or email, update those links when your address changes.

Start with a few clear examples, then add more only when they help a client understand your work. You can [create a Grabbin page](/sign-in) and use your handle as the address to share.
