## Choose the format before the brand

“Link-in-bio tool” can mean a compact list of buttons, a page builder, a storefront, or a full site builder. Start by deciding what someone should understand after opening your profile link. If the answer is simply “open my newest video,” a short list may be enough. If the visitor needs context about your work, choose a format that can show that context too.

## Compare by visitor task

Write down the three actions that matter most, such as viewing a portfolio, listening to a release, or opening your contact page. Then assess each candidate:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Can I show the kind of media my work depends on? | A photographer and a newsletter writer do not need the same first screen. |
| Can I explain what each destination contains? | Useful labels reduce guesswork. |
| Does the mobile page read in a sensible order? | Many visitors arrive from a phone profile. |
| What can I publish on the free plan? | It helps you compare the real starting cost. |
| What happens if I stop paying? | Know whether a domain or feature changes before you rely on it. |

Do not compare a page builder with a full commerce platform as if their feature lists should match. A tool is useful when it supports the job you have, not when it has the most checkboxes.

## When a visual page helps

A link list is efficient when each destination is self-explanatory. A visual page is more useful when an image, video, or short note helps explain why a destination matters. You can also link from that page to a complete portfolio, booking service, shop, or newsletter without trying to recreate those services.

## Run a quick usability check

Share a draft with someone who has not seen your page. Ask them to find one project and one contact destination. If they cannot tell which link to open, rename it or change the order. Finally, tap every link on a phone and confirm that the destination still exists.
