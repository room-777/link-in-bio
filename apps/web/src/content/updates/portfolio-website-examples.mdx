## Look at the visitor's path, not only the screenshot

Portfolio website examples can be inspiring, but a page that looks polished may still leave a visitor unsure what to open. Study what the page explains before each project, how it groups the work, and where it places contact details.

When you review an example, note:

1. **Introduction:** Can you tell what the person does from the first screen?
2. **Selection:** Does the page show a focused set of relevant projects?
3. **Context:** Is the creator's role clear, or do images appear without explanation?
4. **Navigation:** Can a visitor find the full case study or collection?
5. **Next step:** Is the contact or inquiry destination obvious?

These questions apply to photographers, designers, artists, writers, and freelancers, even when their visual styles differ.

## Build a small portfolio before adding everything

Choose three examples that support the kind of work you want to discuss. Give each one a title, a short description, and a link to more detail. Include your role and the scope of the work. If you share client work, use only details and images you have permission to publish.

A bio page can complement a portfolio site by presenting a compact selection and linking visitors into the deeper case studies. It should not pretend to replace a full site when projects need long narratives, filters, or detailed navigation.

## A practical layout to adapt

Start with a short introduction, one featured project, two supporting pieces, and a contact link. On desktop, the featured work can have more visual space. On a phone, confirm the introduction and first project still appear in a sensible order. Use a short text note beside media when the image alone does not explain the work.
