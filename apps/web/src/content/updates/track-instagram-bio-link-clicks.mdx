## A click count does not tell you who clicked

People often ask whether Instagram can show who opened a profile link. A count or aggregate measurement, when available to an account, is not the same as a list of individual visitors. Do not promise that you can identify a person from a link click unless the destination asks them to identify themselves.

Check the current insights available in your Instagram account. The tools and labels can vary. If you need to compare which campaign or profile sent visits, use measurement options provided by your own website or analytics service and follow its privacy rules.

## Compare destinations without collecting unnecessary data

For a simple campaign comparison, use different destination URLs or campaign parameters for each source, then review aggregate visits in the analytics tool already installed on the destination site. Google's [Campaign URL Builder guide](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952) explains how campaign parameters label inbound traffic in Analytics.

That approach can answer questions such as “Did this campaign link get more visits?” It does not reveal a visitor's real-world identity. Avoid collecting personal information you do not need, and tell visitors about analytics where required.

## Keep your profile page honest about measurement

A link page can help organize destinations, but it does not automatically know which named person tapped a link. Grabbin's role is to arrange your links and media on one visual page; use your destination website's existing analytics for aggregate results if available.

Start with a useful question before adding tracking: which destination should be easier to find, or which campaign URL should you compare? If a click count will not change what you do next, you may not need extra tracking.
