An Instagram bio can have **up to 150 characters**, according to the Instagram Help Center. Every letter, space, line break, punctuation mark, and emoji counts toward that limit. Profile links are added separately and do not use any of those 150 characters.

## How many characters can an Instagram bio have?

The bio field takes 150 characters. The same limit applies to personal, creator, and business accounts. Instagram can change its limits, so the counter in your own app is the final word.

| Profile field | Limit | Counts toward the 150? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bio | 150 characters | Yes |
| Username | 30 characters (widely reported; not stated by Instagram) | No |
| Name | No limit stated by Instagram | No |
| Profile links | Up to 5 links | No |
| Spaces and line breaks | 1 character each | Yes |
| Emoji | Usually 1, but flag and combined emoji can take 2 or more | Yes |

## What counts toward the limit

- **Letters, numbers, and punctuation.** Each one is a character.
- **Spaces and line breaks.** A bio stacked over four lines spends characters on every break.
- **Emoji.** Most count as one character, but flags, skin-tone variants, and emoji built from several symbols can count as two or more.
- **Mentions and hashtags.** `@studio` and `#ceramics` are bio text and count in full.

What does not count: your name, your username, and the links you add in the **Links** section of your profile.

## How to check your bio character count

1. **Use the editor's counter.** Open **Edit profile** and tap **Bio**. Instagram shows how many characters you have left as you type, and that is the most reliable count.
2. **Draft in a notes app first.** Write and trim the bio somewhere you can see it all at once, then paste it into Instagram and check the counter.
3. **Leave room for fancy fonts.** Text from "font generator" sites uses special Unicode symbols, and some take more than one character each. Leave a few characters spare and confirm the count after pasting.

## Fit more in 150 characters

Use this order when space is tight:

1. Your role or the type of work you make.
2. A specific subject, audience, or location when it helps.
3. One current next step.

For example: "Independent ceramic artist | Small-batch tableware | New work below." Replace it with your real role and current link.

Cut anything already shown elsewhere. Your username identifies you, so the bio does not need to repeat it. Keep hashtags only when they serve a real discovery purpose. ## Put links in Links, not in the bio text

A web address typed into the bio is not clickable and can eat a large share of your 150 characters. Add it in the profile's **Links** section instead, where you can add up to five. See [how to add a link to your Instagram bio](/update/how-to-add-link-instagram-bio).

If you have more to show than five links can carry, point one of them to a single page with your work, photos, video, and links.
