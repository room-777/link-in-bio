## Check the live counter in Instagram

Instagram's profile editor limits how much bio text you can save. The app's own character counter is the safest check because limits and editing behavior can change. Draft the text in the app and trim it until it saves cleanly; do not rely on a copied character count from an old guide.

## What counts toward the limit

Instagram's bio field has long been capped at 150 characters, and that is the number the app enforces at the time of writing. Treat it as a guide and trust the counter in your app if it differs. A few things use up that space faster than you expect:

- **Spaces and line breaks** count as characters, so a stacked, multi-line bio leaves less room for words.
- **Emoji** count too, and some emoji are stored as more than one character, so a row of icons can cost more than it looks.
- **Mentions and hashtags** such as `@studio` or `#ceramics` are part of the bio text and count in full.

Your name and username are separate fields with their own limits, and profile links sit outside the bio. You do not need to paste a URL into the bio text itself; add it with the profile's link option instead. See [how to add a link to your Instagram bio](/update/how-to-add-link-instagram-bio).

## Keep only the details that help a visitor decide

Use this order when space is tight:

1. Your role or the type of work you make.
2. A specific subject, audience, or location when it helps.
3. One current next step.

For example: “Independent ceramic artist | Small-batch tableware | New work below.” This is a structural example, not a claim or phrase you need to copy. Replace it with your real role and current link.

Remove duplicate information. Your username already identifies your account, so the bio may not need to repeat it. If your name field already says your role, use the bio for a more useful detail. Keep hashtags only when they serve a real discovery purpose; they are not a substitute for a clear description. If you are stuck on wording, browse these [Instagram bio ideas](/update/instagram-bio-ideas) and trim the one closest to your work.

## Use the profile link for the extra context

The bio does not need to list every project or social account. Instagram supports profile links, and a separate page may help when it can show selected work with context. Meta's announcement about adding multiple profile links is [available here](https://about.fb.com/ko/news/2023/04/instagram%EC%9D%B4-%EC%9E%90%EC%9C%A0%EB%A1%9C%EC%9A%B4-%EC%9E%90%EA%B8%B0%ED%91%9C%ED%98%84%EC%9D%84-%EC%84%A0%EB%B3%B4/).

If the visitor needs to browse images, video, and links together, put that detail on the destination page. Keep the bio line concise and make the link label or page heading explain what they will find. See [how to write an Instagram bio](/update/write-instagram-bio) and [how to choose a link-in-bio tool](/update/choose-link-in-bio-tool). If you are new to the idea, [what a link in bio is](/update/what-is-link-in-bio) explains the basics, and the [visual link-in-bio guide](/update/build-visual-link-in-bio) shows how to lay out photos, video, and links as blocks.

## Short bios by type of work

The structure above changes with what you make. A photographer can name a genre and place, then point to recent shoots; the [link-in-bio guide for photographers](/update/link-in-bio-for-photographers) covers what to put on that page. Writers can name a genre or beat and link to a reading list, as in the [guide for writers](/update/link-in-bio-for-writers). Visual artists may want the link to open a small selection of work; see the [artist portfolio guide](/update/artist-portfolio-website).

## Review it as a visitor

Save the bio, open the public profile, and confirm the line breaks, link, and wording still make sense. Recheck it after changing your focus or the page you want people to visit. If you also use TikTok, you can reuse the same short description; [this guide shows how to add your Instagram to your TikTok bio](/update/how-to-add-instagram-to-tiktok-bio).
