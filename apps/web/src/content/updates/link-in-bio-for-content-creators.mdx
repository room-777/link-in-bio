## Give visitors a useful starting point

A content creator may publish videos, articles, photos, or audio across several services. A link-in-bio page is useful when it helps a visitor choose where to go next. Start with your newest or most representative work rather than placing every channel at the top.

## Organize links by what the visitor wants

Use labels that describe the destination: “Watch the full tutorial,” “Read the project notes,” or “See recent photography.” Group the page into a few natural sections if you publish in different formats. Keep a current project separate from an archive so a visitor can tell which item is new.

If you have a newsletter, store, booking page, or paid membership, keep the workflow on the platform that manages it. Link to it with an accurate label. A bio page can point to those destinations, but it should not suggest that it processes a subscription, payment, or booking itself.

## Make the page reflect the content

Use a photo or video when seeing the work helps someone choose. Add a short note when the destination needs context. Keep the page focused enough to review on a phone and remove links that no longer work.
