## Lead with a piece that represents your current work

A writer's link-in-bio page can point to an essay, book, newsletter, or portfolio. Put the destination you most want a new reader to open first, then include an archive or publication profile for people who want more.

Use the title or a plain label that describes the content. “Read the latest essay” is more helpful than “Click here” when that is what the link opens. If the piece is behind a subscription or sign-in, let readers know before they tap.

## Give readers a small amount of context

Add one sentence about the subjects you write about or the type of work you publish. A short introduction can help a reader decide whether to explore, but it does not need to summarize your entire background. Keep credentials and affiliations accurate and current.

## Make several kinds of writing easy to browse

If you write across subjects, group destinations by purpose: recent writing, books, newsletter, and contact. Avoid creating categories that contain only one confusing link. A long archive can remain on your publication site; the bio page can act as a clear entry point.
