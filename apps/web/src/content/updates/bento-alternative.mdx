## Start with what you want to keep

If you are looking for a Bento.me alternative, start by listing what made your page useful. It might be a grid of project images, a few social links, short notes, or the ability to give one piece of work more space than the rest.

Grabbin is an option if you want to arrange links, photos, videos, text, and places on a visual personal page. This guide is written by the team building Grabbin. It explains where our product fits and how to rebuild a page manually; it is not an independent ranking of services.

When we checked [Bento's homepage](https://bento.me) on October 5, 2026, it redirected to Linktree. Check your own old address before relying on it. Creating a Grabbin page does not change where an old Bento address sends visitors.

## Choose a replacement for the job your page does

A similar-looking grid is only one part of the decision. Try the task you will repeat: add a project, adjust its image, check the phone layout, and share the result.

- **For a visual introduction to your work:** try the [Grabbin demo](/demo). It is a sample, so you can explore the layout before creating your own page.
- **For a general link-in-bio platform:** compare the tools described on [Linktree's website](https://linktr.ee/) with the features you actually use.
- **For a standalone one-page website:** [Carrd](https://carrd.co/) offers a one-page site builder. Consider it if you want to build a small site rather than focus on a collection of visual blocks.
- **For selling and managing a creator business:** [Beacons](https://beacons.ai/) describes tools for products, courses, email, and media kits. Check those workflows if they are central to your page.

These are different product directions, not a claim that one service is best for everyone. Check each provider's current features and plan requirements before moving.

## What Grabbin provides

You can add links and media, arrange blocks at different sizes, and use text or section headings to explain what visitors are seeing. Maps can provide context for a public location you want to share.

Desktop and mobile layouts can be edited separately. That lets you give a project room on a larger screen while checking the reading order on a phone.

The free plan includes unlimited links, images, and videos. Pro adds watermark removal and custom domains. Custom domains currently support subdomains such as `portfolio.example.com`, not root domains such as `example.com`. Check the [current plan details](/) before choosing.

## 1. Gather your own content

Collect your project URLs, social profile links, introduction, profile image, and original media files. If you saved a screenshot of your old layout, use it as a visual reference.

Do not depend on the old page to be your only copy. Use files you own or have permission to publish. If you no longer have an image, replace it with a current example rather than presenting an unrelated image as the original project.

This walkthrough uses manual rebuilding. It does not recover an old Bento account, transfer its address, or promise to restore missing files.

## 2. Rebuild the smallest useful page

[Create your Grabbin page](/sign-in), add your introduction, and start with one featured project. Add a project image, a short explanation of your role, and a link to the full work.

Then add supporting profiles and a contact destination. You can bring back more content later, but the first version should already explain who you are and where a visitor should go next.

For example, a designer might rebuild a six-block page as an introduction, a large project image, a case-study link, a second project, an availability note, and a contact link. This is an illustrative arrangement, not a customer result. The [designer page guide](/update/link-in-bio-for-designers) explains that structure in more detail.

## 3. Recheck the layout on a phone

Do not assume that copying a desktop arrangement preserves the mobile experience. Switch to the mobile layout, check the block order, and adjust image crops where necessary.

Keep the explanation close to the work it describes. Open the public page on a phone and follow each project and contact link. The [step-by-step building guide](/update/build-visual-link-in-bio) covers adding blocks, saving, and sharing.

## 4. Replace links you control

Once the new page is ready, update the places where you shared the old address:

- Social profile links and pinned posts.
- Email signatures and portfolio introductions.
- Profiles on communities or directories you can edit.
- Digital business cards and downloadable documents.
- QR codes that point directly to the old address.

A printed QR code still contains its original destination. Test it before reusing it; a new profile elsewhere does not automatically update that code. For an old link on someone else's website, ask the site owner to replace it.

## 5. Keep an address and content you can manage

You can share your Grabbin handle, or connect a subdomain you own with Pro. Keeping control of your own domain gives you the option to change where that address points later. It does not move your page content for you, so keep copies of your media and important text too.

Follow the [custom domain setup guide](/update/connect-custom-domain) if you choose that route. Confirm the secure connection works before replacing your shared links.

## Try the page before committing to the move

Build a small version with your own content, check both layouts, and share it with someone who knows your work. If the page makes your work easier to find and feels comfortable to maintain, expand it from there.
