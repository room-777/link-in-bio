## Use a portfolio template as a starting structure

A portfolio website template should help organize information, not force every person's work into the same look. Start with the order a visitor needs: understand who you are, see relevant work, learn what you contributed, and find a next step.

### A simple one-page outline

1. **Introduction:** Your name, role, and one sentence about your focus.
2. **Featured project:** The work you most want a visitor to open.
3. **Selected work:** A few additional examples that show range or depth.
4. **Context:** Your role, medium, client, or process when it is appropriate to share.
5. **Contact:** An email address, existing form, or professional profile.

Use only sections that support your work. A photographer may need a larger image; a writer may need a short excerpt; a product designer may need a case-study link and a description of their role.

## Keep the full work in the right place

If a project needs a long explanation, keep the case study on your website or portfolio service and link to it. A compact page can introduce the work, show a visual, and help a visitor decide whether to continue. Do not reduce a detailed project to a thumbnail with no explanation.

Grabbin is a visual page builder, not a full portfolio CMS. It supports links, photos, videos, text, and places in flexible blocks. Use it for a short shareable overview and keep large collections or detailed case studies in the service designed for them.

## Check the template at phone size

Open the published page on a phone and read from top to bottom. Is the first item still the right one? Do images crop sensibly? Is the contact route visible without making the visitor scan a long list?
