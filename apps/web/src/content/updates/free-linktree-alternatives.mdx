## “Free” matters only when the plan covers your real use

A free badge alone does not tell you whether a link-in-bio service fits. Compare what you can publish and share without paying, then check the terms before moving an audience to it.

## Use this free-plan checklist

- **Page access:** Can you publish a page and keep it available on the free plan?
- **Useful content:** Can you add the links and media your page needs?
- **Appearance:** Can you make the page clear and recognizably yours, or is customization restricted?
- **Address:** Is the included URL acceptable for the profiles where you will use it?
- **Limits:** Are there caps on pages, blocks, or uploads that affect your current plan?
- **Upgrade triggers:** Which features require payment, and would you need them now?

Read the current plan page for each service. Product plans change; an old comparison article can be wrong even if its advice sounds confident. Do not assume that “free” means unlimited, ad-free, or permanent unless the service states it.

## Decide what the page must do

If you only need a few destinations, Instagram itself allows up to five profile links. If you want a single page that combines links with photos, videos, short text, or places, compare visual page builders instead. A full portfolio site may be a better fit when you need long case studies, detailed navigation, or a complete domain-based website.

Grabbin offers a free way to start a visual page. Its blocks can bring media and links together, while Pro includes a custom-domain option. It does not provide built-in booking, lead forms, or checkout, so keep those steps with the external services you already use.

## Which page format fits your work

People looking for a Linktree alternative may want a different layout, a place for selected work, or a simpler way to present several parts of their online identity. Look at four things in the actual published page:

1. **Presentation:** Is it a short list, a full website, or a flexible visual page?
2. **Content:** Can you show the media and context visitors need, or only link labels?
3. **Next step:** Does a visitor know which link to open or how to contact you?
4. **Ownership:** Can you use a stable address that you are comfortable sharing?

A musician promoting one new release may be well served by a clean link list. A photographer may need a strong image, a small set of galleries, and links to booking or contact pages. A freelancer may need a short service description beside selected work. A creator with a large catalogue may need a full site or a storefront. See the guides for [photographers](/update/link-in-bio-for-photographers) and [writers](/update/link-in-bio-for-writers), and the [visual link-in-bio guide](/update/build-visual-link-in-bio).

## Make a fair comparison in one sitting

Choose one representative project and add only the information a visitor needs: a short introduction, one featured item, a few destinations, and one clear next step. Preview each candidate on a phone. Note any branding, missing block type, or plan limit that changes the visitor's experience.
