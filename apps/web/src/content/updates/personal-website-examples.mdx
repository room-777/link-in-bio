## Notice what each page helps the visitor understand

When browsing personal website examples, look beyond color and animation. Ask whether the first screen explains who owns the page, what they do, and what is worth opening. A useful example gives the visitor context before asking them to explore.

Take notes on the parts you can adapt:

1. A concise introduction that identifies the person's work.
2. A featured item that shows the work rather than merely describing it.
3. Short explanations that make project links understandable.
4. A contact route or next step that is easy to find.

Keep the details that fit your own goal. A writer may lead with an essay; an artist may lead with a current series; a freelancer may lead with two relevant client projects.

## Choose a page size that matches the material

A compact page works when you want to introduce yourself and direct people to a few destinations. A full personal website is a better fit when you need a biography, multiple project pages, articles, and deeper navigation. You do not need to move every part of your online presence into one tool.

## Build a useful version before polishing it

Write the introduction, choose the projects, and add the links first. Then improve spacing, imagery, and color. This order helps you notice when a beautiful layout has no clear message or when a page contains too many equal-priority choices.
