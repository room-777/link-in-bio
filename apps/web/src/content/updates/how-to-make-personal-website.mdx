## Decide what the site should help someone do

Before picking a builder, finish this sentence: “After opening my page, I want a visitor to ___.” Possible answers include viewing selected work, reading a current article, contacting you, or choosing between several destinations. One clear purpose makes later decisions easier.

## Gather the content first

Collect a short introduction, two or three relevant examples, the links to their full details, and a contact route. Write a sentence for each project explaining what it is and what you contributed. Only share client names, images, and outcomes when you have permission and evidence.

## Choose the smallest format that fits

A compact personal page can introduce you and point people to deeper material. A full site is more appropriate when you need long case studies, categories, article archives, or complex navigation. You can use both: a short page as the public starting point and an existing website for the long-form work.

Grabbin offers a visual page with blocks for text, photos, videos, links, and places. It does not host booking, checkout, or a full article archive. Keep those tasks on the service already designed for them and link there.

## Publish, then test the visitor journey

Check the page on a phone. Read it from top to bottom, open each link, and verify that the first project still matches your current focus. Ask a person unfamiliar with your work to find one project and one way to contact you. Use their confusion as a signal to edit labels or order.
